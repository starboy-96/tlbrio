Your brand guidelines are worthless if your team ignores them. You've probably seen it: a sales director uses Helvetica instead of your brand font. Marketing adds an unapproved colour. Someone embeds a 5MB logo. By the time 100 people are building decks, you're not managing a presentation standard any more. You're managing chaos.

The problem is simple. You can't review every deck. You don't have time to fix typos and spacing errors across 50 quarterly business reviews. And your team doesn't want to wait for approval before they pitch.

So stop trying to police every slide. Instead, make it harder to break the rules than to follow them.

**Start with a locked template.** Not a suggestion or a guide. A PowerPoint file where the master slides are protected and only your brand fonts, colours, and spacing exist. When someone opens it, they cannot change the core elements. They can only fill in their content. This removes 80% of the variance immediately.

Next, distribute this as the only template your team uses. Store it somewhere central, not scattered across shared drives. Make it the default when someone opens PowerPoint. [Use a tool that enforces consistent templates across your organisation](https://tlbr.io/#features) so updates apply instantly to everyone, not six months later when someone actually checks email.

Third, train your team on what they can actually change. Show them which text boxes are flexible. Show them where they can add images and why margins matter. Give them guardrails, not restrictions. People comply better when they understand why.

For larger teams, assign brand champions in each department. Not gatekeepers. Champions who know the guidelines, answer questions, and catch egregious errors before decks go out. This gives you distributed oversight without centralised bottlenecks.

Finally, audit quarterly. Pull 10 random decks. Look for drift. When you spot it, share what you found with the whole team. Not as punishment. As learning. "We're seeing some off-brand fonts appearing. Here's why consistency matters. Here's how to fix it."

Your brand standards scale when you remove friction from following them. Make the right choice the easy choice. Your sales team will thank you.
