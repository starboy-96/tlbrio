# Keep brand standards in PowerPoint—even with 100+ creators

You’ve got 100+ people in your organisation building PowerPoint decks every week. Some are in marketing, others in finance, and a few in remote offices. One designer can’t babysit every slide. Yet your CEO still expects slides that look like *your* brand, not like a loose collection of accidental fonts and clashing colours.

It’s not about control—it’s about clarity. When every deck echoes your brand, your message lands harder. So how do you scale consistency without turning PowerPoint into a corporate straitjacket?

## Start with a living brand guide in PowerPoint

Most brand guides live in PDFs gathering dust. Move yours into PowerPoint itself.

Drop your approved fonts, colours, and logo treatments into a master deck. Name every slide logically: “Brand_Colours_Dark”, “Brand_Font_H1”, “Brand_Logo_Lockup”. Store this file in a shared OneDrive or SharePoint folder labelled “Brand Templates”.

Update this deck every quarter—no more waiting for the next rebrand. When someone opens it, they see your brand alive, not trapped in a 200-page PDF.

## Use a template that actually works

Static templates don’t survive real workflows. Build a flexible template that lets teams swap hero images or change data colours without breaking the grid.

Start with a clean master slide. Set up a 16:9 layout with a fixed header, dynamic footer, and a content-safe zone. Use section headers in consistent styling, and lock them so no one drags them off-canvas.

Then create *variants*—light, dark, minimal. Each variant inherits the same colour palette and font rules. You preserve brand identity while giving teams the freedom to adapt.

## Automate the boring bits

Manual font checks and colour sampling waste hours every week. Automate them.

Use PowerPoint’s built-in “Check Accessibility” tool to flag suspect fonts or contrast issues. But don’t stop there. Pair it with a lightweight add-in that scans every deck for rogue colours or misused logos.

Run these scans weekly. Flag offenders in a shared Teams channel: “Finance deck used the old blue. Please update.” Over time, the system becomes a gentle nudge, not a scolding.

## Keep the brand alive, not locked down

Rigid templates breed workarounds. Designers start exporting slides to Photoshop, breaking the whole workflow.

Instead, give teams *brand tokens*—small, reusable elements they can slot in without fear. A token could be a pre-styled data bar, a branded callout box, or a colour swatch group.

Keep tokens in a dedicated slide in your master deck. Update one token, and every deck using it updates automatically. Teams stay creative. Your brand stays intact.

## Make brand checks part of the workflow

Add a brand review step to your deck handoff. Before a deck ships, run a 60-second scan using your automated tool. If it passes, green-light it. If not, fix it before the client sees it.

You can even bake this into your PowerPoint ribbon. A single click runs the full audit and drops a summary slide at the end. Reviewers see the results instantly, not buried in a 40-page feedback doc.

## Train the team, then trust them

Run a 30-minute session on your brand system. Show them where to find tokens, how to update the master, and why rogue fonts hurt credibility. Make it visual—show a before-and-after of a slide that went from “meh” to “yours”.

Then step back. Trust your teams to adapt the templates, not ignore them.

## Measure what matters

Track two things:

1. **Template adoption rate**—how many decks start from your branded template?
2. **Brand violations per month**—are rogue colours decreasing?

If adoption is low, your templates might be too rigid. If violations persist, your training isn’t landing. Adjust fast.

## The takeaway you can use today

Take one hour this week to set up a living brand guide in PowerPoint. Build a flexible template with variants and tokens. Then run a single automated scan on your last five decks.

You’ll spot inconsistencies immediately. More importantly, you’ll prove to your team that brand consistency doesn’t require bureaucracy—just the right tools and a little automation.

Your CEO will notice the difference. Your clients will too.
