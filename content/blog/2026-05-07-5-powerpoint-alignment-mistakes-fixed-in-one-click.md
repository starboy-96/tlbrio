# You’re wasting hours on alignment in PowerPoint

Open your last deck and squint at the left margin of every slide. I bet at least three text boxes sit slightly off-grid. That’s not designer perfection. That’s messy inconsistency hurting your brand.

Alignment isn’t decoration. It’s the invisible grid that holds your deck together. Get it wrong and your audience feels the tension, even if they can’t say why. But you don’t have to eyeball it for hours. These five alignment mistakes cripple most enterprise decks. Each one can be fixed in one click once you know where to look.

## Text boxes aren’t snapped to the grid

You drag a text box onto the slide and let it float. It settles somewhere between grid lines. The next box you add? It lands on a different line. After 20 slides you’ve created a visual staircase.

PowerPoint’s grid helps here, but most people ignore it. Turn it on: View > Grid Settings > Snap objects to grid. Now any object you drag snaps to the nearest grid line. No more visual drift.

## Icons sit at random baselines

Icons look crisp on their own. In a bulleted list they turn sloppy. The icon baseline wobbles above or below the text. The eye picks up the mismatch immediately.

Select the whole list. Use Align > Align Bottom to anchor every icon to the same baseline. One click, instant consistency.

## Bullet indents wander across slides

You copy a slide and paste it elsewhere. The bullets inherit the new slide’s ruler settings. Suddenly indentation is wider or narrower. The deck feels fragmented.

Reset it fast. Select the text. Open Home > Paragraph > Increase Indent or Decrease Indent until the bullets align with your master style. Then update the master so it never happens again.

## Images break the margin rule

Images often sit flush against the slide edge. Other objects respect a 10mm margin. The margin break jars the viewer.

Select the image. Hold Shift and drag the corner to resize while preserving proportions. Drag it inward until it snaps to the margin guide. One action, one click less.

## Charts aren’t centre-aligned in placeholders

You drop a chart into a titled placeholder. It drifts 2mm to the left. The title above it stays centred. The misalignment screams amateur.

Right-click the chart. Choose Position > Align > Align Centre. It snaps to the placeholder centre instantly. No more tugging with the mouse.
