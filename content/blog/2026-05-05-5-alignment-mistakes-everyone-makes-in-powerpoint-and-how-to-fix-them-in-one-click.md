## Why alignment still trips up even experienced teams

Alignment isn’t just about aesthetics — it’s a proxy for professionalism. Misaligned text boxes, uneven bullet spacing, or off-centre charts scream “rushed” before your audience reads a single word. And yet, despite PowerPoint’s built-in tools, most teams still waste minutes tweaking positions pixel by pixel.

The good news? All five of the most common alignment mistakes can be fixed in one click — if you know where to look.

## Mistake 1: Sprawling bullets that don’t line up

Nothing says “amateur hour” faster than bulleted lists where the second line starts halfway across the slide. Even when you set consistent indentation, PowerPoint sometimes ignores it when text wraps.

**Fix it in one click:**
Select the text box, go to **Home → Paragraph → Increase Indent** or **Decrease Indent**. PowerPoint will snap the wrapped lines into alignment with the first line. No more dragging.

## Mistake 2: Charts that drift from their labels

A chart’s title sits perfectly centred above the data — until you resize the slide or adjust margins. Suddenly, the chart has wandered, and the title now floats above empty space.

**Fix it in one click:**
Select both the chart and its title, then click **Format → Align → Align Centre**. PowerPoint will centre them together, treating the pair as a single unit. Lock them with a grouped object to prevent future drift.

## Mistake 3: Icons and text that refuse to play nice

You drop in a neat icon from the icon library, only to find it’s slightly higher or lower than the adjacent text. Manual nudging leads to inconsistent spacing across slides.

**Fix it in one click:**
Select the icon and text together, then click **Format → Align → Align Middle**. The icon will snap to the vertical centre of the text line, ensuring visual harmony.

## Mistake 4: Margins that vanish when you copy slides

You’ve spent time aligning objects to your brand margins — only for the next slide to inherit misaligned elements because margins weren’t preserved during copy-paste.

**Fix it in one click:**
Use **View → Slide Master** to set permanent guides. Any new slide based on that master will preserve your alignment. Update once, maintain everywhere.

## Mistake 5: Inconsistent spacing between sections

Teams often add blank space between sections using manual line breaks or extra text boxes. Over time, this creates uneven gaps that look sloppy.

**Fix it in one click:**
Select all relevant objects, then click **Format → Align → Distribute Vertically** or **Distribute Horizontally**. PowerPoint redistributes space evenly between them in a single click.

## The one-click workflow for alignment sanity

1. **Select multiple objects** (Ctrl/Cmd + click).
2. Use **Align** and **Distribute** in the **Format** tab.
3. Group related elements (**Format → Group**) to lock alignment.
4. Save a **Slide Master** layout for reuse.
5. Reapply alignment across decks with one click.

## Stop polishing, start presenting

Alignment isn’t creative — it’s compliance. Your brand guidelines exist for a reason. PowerPoint gives you the tools to enforce them instantly. The next time your slide looks “almost right,” resist the impulse to nudge. One click, done.
