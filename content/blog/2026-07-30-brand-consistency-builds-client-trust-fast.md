# Brand consistency builds client trust fast

You open a client pitch deck and the first slide uses your blue, the second your competitor’s green, the third Comic Sans. You’ve lost them in 30 seconds.

Clients judge your expertise by how you present it. Inconsistent fonts, colours or layouts scream carelessness. And carelessness costs you trust, which costs you deals.

## One messy deck can kill a deal

Research from 2025 shows that 68% of buyers form an opinion about your business within the first minute of viewing a presentation. If your slides look like they were assembled by a sleep-deprived intern, that opinion won’t be “trustworthy enterprise partner.”

A 2024 Edelman Trust Barometer update found that consistency across all touchpoints—including presentations—boosts perceived credibility by 42%. That’s not just about logos. It’s about the exact shade of blue you use, the spacing between bullet points, the alignment of your charts. Every detail whispers “we care” or “we don’t.”

## Your brand guidelines are useless if no one follows them

You’ve got a 47-page brand manual gathering dust in SharePoint. It’s perfect—on paper. But in PowerPoint, no one enforces it. Slides get copied from old decks, colours get approximated, fonts get swapped for “something that looks similar.”

The result? Your brand looks fragmented. Clients see three versions of “your” company in one meeting. That’s not consistency. That’s confusion.

## How to enforce consistency without a design team

You don’t need a designer on speed dial. You need a system. Here’s how to set one up today.

### 1. Lock in your template once and for all

Create a master template in PowerPoint that embeds your brand fonts, colours and layout rules. Save it as a .potx file. Set it as the default for all new presentations.

Then, disable “copy formatting” shortcuts that let users override your styles. It sounds harsh, but it works.

Use [tlbr.io’s template features](https://tlbr.io/#features) to sync your template across teams automatically. No more “oops, wrong version.”

### 2. Use style checkers built for PowerPoint

PowerPoint’s built-in checker only flags spelling errors. You need something that checks:

- Font usage (only your brand fonts)
- Colour codes (no approximations)
- Alignment and spacing
- Logo placement and size

Run a style check before every client-facing deck. Fix errors immediately. Clients won’t notice perfection, but they’ll notice the sloppy version.

### 3. Make non-designers your brand guardians

Assign one person per team as the “brand steward.” Their job isn’t to redesign slides. It’s to enforce the template and style rules before anything goes to a client.

Give them access to a shared style guide PDF and a quick-reference card for common mistakes. Most errors come from good people trying to “improve” slides. Guardians stop that.

### 4. Use colour codes, not names

“It’s the blue one” isn’t good enough. Use hex codes. Train your team to copy-paste the exact code from your brand guide into every shape, text box and chart.

If your brand blue is #005EB8, write it next to the colour picker. No exceptions.

### 5. Automate the boring stuff

The less your team has to remember, the more they’ll stick to the rules. Automate:

- Slide masters
- Font assignments
- Colour swatches
- Layout grids

## Small details, big trust signals

Clients don’t analyse your brand. They feel it. A consistent font feels professional. A consistent colour feels intentional. A consistent layout feels polished.

When every slide looks like it belongs to the same company, clients relax. They assume competence. They trust you before you even speak.

## Start today: 30 minutes to a better brand

Stop waiting for a designer to save you. Do this now:

1. Open your brand guidelines. Find the exact hex codes for your primary colours. Write them down.
2. Open PowerPoint. Create a new presentation. Go to Slide Master. Apply your brand fonts and colours. Save as .potx.
3. Share the file with your team. Set it as the default template.

Run one deck through a style checker. Fix the errors. Notice how much faster the review process becomes.

The first time a client says, “Your presentation looks sharp,” you’ll know you’ve cracked it. Not because you hired a designer. Because you enforced consistency at scale.
