## Slide titles that look drunk

Open any deck that’s been edited by three people and you’ll find slide titles that stagger across the slide like they’ve had one too many. The text is centred but the left edge is three pixels left of where it should be. The spacing between letters is inconsistent. It looks sloppy because it is.

This happens when you copy slides, paste text, or resize placeholders without resetting the baseline. PowerPoint remembers the last alignment for each text box, but forgets to tell you. You end up nudging objects by hand for five minutes, only to find the spacing is still wrong at 90% zoom.

Stop squinting at the ruler. Grab your title text box, click the Align Centre button, then click Distribute Vertically. The title snaps back to the slide’s horizontal centre and the baseline aligns with the grid. One click, done.

## Charts drifting into no-man’s-land

You drop a bar chart onto the slide and it drifts three centimetres right because the default anchor is set to the top-left corner. The gridlines no longer match the slide master. The legend overlaps the right margin.

Most users drag the chart back into place, eyeballing the gap. You lose precision and waste thirty seconds every time.

Set the chart’s anchor to the slide centre before you resize. Click the chart, go to Format > Size & Properties > Position, and choose “Centre” from the dropdown. Resize the chart by holding Shift while you drag a corner. It stays centred and the gaps to the edges remain equal. Slide layout preserved.

## Bullet points playing hide and seek

Bullet points start flush left but after a few edits they wander off by themselves. One indent is two spaces, the next is three. The text box expands to fit the longest line, pushing other bullets to the second row.

This is the curse of “Keep Text Flat.” PowerPoint remembers the last text wrap and applies it to every paste without asking.

Select all the bullet text, press Ctrl+Shift+Z to undo any manual spacing, then right-click the text box > Format Shape > Text Options > Text Box. Set “Wrap text in shape” to OFF. Every line now respects the bullet indentation. The text box collapses to the smallest possible width and all lines sit where they should.

## Logos that forget they’re guests

Your company logo sits in the top-right corner of every slide. You paste it into a new layout and suddenly it’s 12 pixels lower than the master. You nudge it up, only for the next edit to move it back down.

Logos are usually inserted as pictures, not placeholders. They don’t inherit the master’s position rules.

Convert the logo to a picture placeholder in Slide Master view. Click View > Slide Master, drag the logo into the placeholder, then set the placeholder to “Match Other Objects.” Now any slide using that layout snaps the logo to the exact position, every time.

## Gridlines ignored because you’re busy

You swear you’ve enabled gridlines, but the shapes still overlap by two pixels. The slide looks messy but you can’t see the gap until you print it or project it on a big screen.

PowerPoint’s gridlines are too faint and stop at 50% zoom. You need a stronger cue.

Go to View > Grid Settings > Grid and Guides. Increase the gridline spacing to 10 pixels, set the spacing to 5 pixels, and check “Display smart guides when shapes are aligned.” Now every shape snaps to the nearest grid intersection and you see the gap before it becomes visible on screen.

## One-click fix for tomorrow’s deck

Bookmark this routine. Before you export any deck:

1. Press Ctrl+A to select everything.
2. Press Ctrl+Shift+G to group all objects temporarily.
3. Go to the tlbr.io add-in and click “Align All.”
4. Choose “Grid Alignment” and set the tolerance to 2 pixels.
5. Click Apply. All objects snap to the nearest grid intersection in one second.

You’ve just fixed every alignment mistake in the deck with one click. Close the add-in, ungroup, and export. The deck is now consistent, grid-perfect, and ready for the boardroom.
