Picture an HR adviser on a Thursday afternoon with a wellbeing briefing due on Friday morning. She opens the shared folder, finds a file called "HR template FINAL v3" and starts typing over last quarter's slides. The header bar is an older shade of the brand blue. The body text is in a font the company stopped using some time ago. The logo sits in the bottom left, where it used to go before the rebrand.

None of this is her fault. She isn't a designer, the brand guide is a PDF she has never needed to open, and the template she used looked official. She copies two charts from a finance deck, which bring their own colours with them, and pastes a table from Word that arrives in Calibri. By five o'clock the deck is finished, and it looks like three different companies made it.

Multiply that by recruitment campaigns, policy updates and onboarding packs, all changing week to week, and HR quietly becomes one of the places a brand drifts furthest from its guidelines.

## What changes when the brand lives inside PowerPoint

- **One current template replaces the folder of old ones.** A single up-to-date .potx file, with the correct theme colours, theme fonts and logo position set on the slide master, means every new deck starts from the right place rather than from whatever was edited last.
- **Approved slide types are ready to use.** Instead of reshaping a blank slide, the adviser picks a layout built for the job, such as a policy summary, a timeline or a key-dates slide, and replaces the placeholder text.
- **Pasted content gets brought back on brand quickly.** With brand colours and table and chart formatting available in one click, a chart from another team's deck can be restyled in seconds rather than left as it arrived. A bespoke toolbar like [tlbr.io](https://tlbr.io/#features) puts these commands in one place.
- **The design team stops being the repair service.** When the starting point is right and corrections are easy, fewer decks need rescuing the night before they are presented.

The pattern isn't unique to HR. It shows up wherever people who aren't designers produce slides under time pressure. A useful first step is to open the folder your HR colleagues actually use, look at the template they reach for, and compare its slide master with the current brand guide. The differences you find there are the ones repeating in every deck.
