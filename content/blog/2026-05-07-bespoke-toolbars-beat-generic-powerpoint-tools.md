# Bespoke toolbars beat generic PowerPoint tools

Your team spends hours every week tweaking fonts, colours and layouts in PowerPoint. And every tweak risks breaking the brand guidelines you painstakingly created.

Generic tools can’t prevent that. Bespoke toolbars can.

## Why generic toolbars fail enterprise teams

Generic toolbars give you the same buttons everyone else gets: bold, italic, centre-align. They don’t know your brand’s Pantone 485 red from your blue-grey 80. They won’t stop someone overwriting your corporate font with Calibri.

And when your annual report lands in the CEO’s inbox with the wrong accent colour, it’s not just a typo. It’s a brand breach.

## What a bespoke toolbar actually does

A bespoke toolbar does three things generic toolbars can’t:

1. **Enforces brand rules in real time**
   You set the hex codes, fonts and layout constraints once. The toolbar applies them automatically. If someone tries to use Comic Sans, the toolbar greys out the option.

2. **Slashes production time**
   Your team stops hunting for the right settings. One click applies your approved colour palette, headline style and footer layout. What used to take minutes now takes seconds.

3. **Keeps decks consistent across offices**
   A London designer and a Singapore analyst both see the same toolbar options. No more “it looked fine on my machine” excuses.

## The hidden cost of “good enough” toolbars

Every time a teammate overrides a brand rule, you pay. First in rework. Then in lost credibility. In the worst cases, in regulatory risk when a deck misrepresents data.

A McKinsey study found that poor presentation quality costs large firms an average of £120,000 per year in lost bids and delayed decisions. Most of that waste starts with a font that’s “almost right.”

## How to build a toolbar that actually works

Start small. Pick the three brand violations that happen most often. Maybe it’s slide masters, maybe it’s footers. Build one toolbar to rule them.

Train your team for one hour. Show them how to use the toolbar, not how to ignore it. Put a 30-second video on the intranet. Make it idiot-proof.

Measure what you fix. Track how many brand breaches drop to zero after you launch the toolbar. Share the numbers with leadership. That’s how you prove the ROI.

## Tomorrow’s fix starts today

You don’t need another style guide gathering dust in SharePoint. You need a toolbar that fights for your brand every time someone opens PowerPoint.

Set up a 15-minute session this afternoon. Open PowerPoint, open your brand guide, and start building. The first version won’t be perfect. It only needs to stop the most common mistakes.

Do that, and you’ll reclaim hours every week. More importantly, you’ll reclaim the trust that comes from decks that look like one team, not a hundred freelancers.
