Nobody budgets time for fixing fonts. It turns up anyway, usually on a Thursday afternoon, when a deck lands for review and half the slides have drifted from the brand.

The causes are rarely dramatic. A slide copied from last year's pitch still carries the old typeface. A chart pasted from Excel arrives in Excel's default colours. Someone nudges the corporate blue by eye until it looks close enough. Each problem takes a few minutes to put right, which is exactly why nobody tracks it.

Fixing is the visible part. The repetition is where the time actually goes. A reviewer corrects a colour on one slide, but the old shade survives in a theme or a saved file, and the same mistake comes back in next month's deck. The team ends up correcting identical errors on a loop, and the person doing it is often the most senior one in the room.

A memo will not break that loop.

What helps is making the right choice the easiest one: approved fonts and colours sitting where people already work, layouts that start correct, and tidying that takes a click rather than ten minutes of nudging. When the default is on-brand, review goes back to being about the argument on the slide.

Try this on your next review. Keep a tally of every formatting fix you make, and note whether you have made the same fix before. If the repeats outnumber the one-offs, the problem is the setup, and that is where to start.
