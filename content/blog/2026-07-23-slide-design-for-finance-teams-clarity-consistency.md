# Slide design for finance teams: clarity, consistency, credibility

Your quarterly results deck landed in the CFO’s inbox at 10:03 p.m. on a Tuesday. By 10:05, you had a reply: “Can we redo slide 17? The blue chart isn’t matching the brand palette and the numbers feel off-centre.” Sound familiar? Finance teams spend weeks crunching data, only to lose credibility over misaligned pixels and font mismatches. A sharp-looking slide isn’t vanity—it’s trust in visual form.

## Start with a style guide—your silent credibility booster

Finance audiences expect precision. A style guide turns vague requests like “make it pop” into repeatable rules. Start with one.

Pick two primary fonts: one for headings, one for body text. Arial Bold 44pt for slide titles and Calibri 28pt for labels keep decks readable on a boardroom screen. Then lock in your colours. Use your company’s exact hex codes for the blue that appears on investor relations pages. No eyeballing. No “close enough.”

And test your guide. Open a random slide deck from 2025. If the font switches to Times New Roman on slide 8, your style guide isn’t guiding anything.

## Build templates that do the heavy lifting

You shouldn’t format every slide manually. Build a template once that enforces the right layout, spacing and data placement. Title slides always have the same logo position. Table slides maintain 1.5 line spacing for readability. Waterfall charts inherit the brand palette automatically.

Use a PowerPoint add-in that syncs these templates across your team. When the CFO sends the next deck request, anyone on your team can open the template, drop in fresh data, and hit send—without hunting for the right font or colour picker.

## Data, done right—clear, centred, credible

Finance slides live or die on data. A table jammed with 14 columns screams “spreadsheet vomit.” Trim ruthlessly. No more than seven rows. Round decimals to one decimal place. Use bold for the key KPI, never italics. Centre-align numbers under their headers so the eye tracks down, not sideways.

Charts need breathing room. Slide margins should be at least 20mm. A 10% drop in margin may look small on a busy slide, but it’s enough to make a regional director squint. Give charts their own slide with a crisp title like “EMEA Revenues Q2 2026 — 10% decline vs prior year.”

And label everything. A grey bar labelled “Other” invites questions. A grey bar labelled “Other (includes restructuring costs)” answers them before they’re asked.

## Consistency across teams—no more rogue decks

When finance teams work in silos, decks drift. Marketing uses Pantone 300 C, treasury uses RGB 0,51,102, and tax uses “that nice navy”. The CEO sees three shades of blue and assumes sloppy work.

Create one source of truth. Use a branded PowerPoint template that lives in a shared OneDrive folder. When updates happen—say, the logo rotates—push the change to the template. Every new deck starts with the fresh asset. No more version chaos.

## Proof before you present

Before you send, run a quick credibility scan. Check every slide.

Does the title match the content? A slide titled “EBITDA Improvement” with a flat line chart fails the sniff test. Are the numbers aligned? Misaligned decimals erode trust faster than an off-by-one error in the model.

Use the built-in PowerPoint accessibility checker. It flags colour contrast issues that could trip up colour-blind stakeholders. Fix them before the deck leaves your screen.

## One action you can take today

Open your most recent finance deck. Count how many font families appear. If it’s more than two, it’s time to create a style guide and a branded template. Lock it down, push it out, and watch the revision rounds drop.
