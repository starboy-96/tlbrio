# ARTICLE

## Before you send that presentation, run this evidence check

Check whether your key claims are actually supported before stakeholders spot gaps.

---

Your sales deck states revenue will grow 15%. A finance presentation forecasts margins will improve. A project update promises faster delivery.

Does each claim rest on evidence the slide actually shows? Or is it stated but unsupported—leaving stakeholders uncertain whether you've thought it through?

Weak evidence doesn't always survive first contact with a stakeholder. Catching it yourself costs minutes now. Hearing it in a live presentation or email costs hours of clarification later.

Before you circulate, ask these five questions about each major claim:

**1. Is the claim stated as a single sentence?**
If you have to paraphrase to explain what you're claiming, rewrite the slide text. Vague claims invite vague feedback and weak decisions.

**2. Is evidence shown on the slide?**
A number, chart, quote or example should appear—not just be mentioned. If it's only in your speaker notes, stakeholders won't see it.

**3. Does the evidence actually support the claim?**
A chart showing correlation is not evidence of causation. Market growth is not evidence your product caused it. Three examples are not a trend. If the logic gap would take 30 seconds to explain, your slide needs more evidence or a narrower claim.

**4. Are numbers contextualised?**
A 20% increase is meaningless without knowing the baseline, timeframe or industry norm. 20% of what? Compared to when? Better than competitors? Add the context or the number proves nothing.

**5. Are assumptions named?**
If your forecast assumes the market stays stable, say so. If a quote came from one customer type, flag it. Stakeholders spot buried assumptions fast—and distrust decks where they're hidden.

If you answer "no" to any question, that's a slide to fix before sharing.

This takes 90 seconds per slide. It prevents the longer round of revision that comes when a stakeholder requests evidence you haven't shown.
