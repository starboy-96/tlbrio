# Pitch decks that win legal clients

Your pitch deck isn’t a document. It’s a closing tool. Yet most law firms still hand over slides that look like internal memos with a logo slapped on top. The client opens it on a tablet during a taxi ride and swipes away within 10 seconds. If your deck doesn’t grip attention immediately and maintain it, you’ve lost before you’ve even explained your approach.

## Ditch the dense paragraphs

Professional services decks are full of text because partners think every detail must be justified. The result? Slides that look like an email from counsel. Clients won’t read 80-word paragraphs about “leveraging synergies.” They want clarity, not compliance.

Break text into short, scannable statements. Use bullet points only when you need to show hierarchy or steps. If you must include a paragraph, keep it under three lines. And use the slide title to summarise the point in plain English.

## Start with a problem, not a pedigree

Your opening slide probably lists founding year, offices, and rankings. Clients skim past that. They care about their problem, not your heritage.

Begin with a slide titled: “What keeps you up at night?” Use one sentence and one data point. For example:

> “78% of GCs lose sleep over regulatory changes in ESG reporting.”

Follow it with a single solution slide: “We turn uncertainty into compliance.” Now the client is listening.

## Visuals beat bullet points

Lawyers love bullet points. Clients hate them. A slide full of dashes feels like a compliance checklist. Instead, use visuals that tell the story.

- Replace a list of “our services” with a simple Venn diagram showing where your expertise overlaps the client’s industry pain points.
- Turn a timeline of past cases into a clean horizontal bar chart with key outcomes highlighted in your brand colour.
- Use a before/after graphic to show the impact of your regulatory advice.

## Slide count matters more than you think

A pitch deck for a £50m mandate doesn’t need 30 slides. It needs 12. Anything longer signals disorganisation. Clients assume if you can’t edit your own story, you can’t edit theirs.

Start with a 10-slide structure:

1. Problem
2. Why now
3. Our insight
4. Our approach
5. Case study (one)
6. Why us (three bullets max)
7. Timeline
8. Investment
9. Team (two key bios)
10. Next steps

Trim ruthlessly. If a slide doesn’t lead directly to a signature, cut it.

## Brand consistency is your silent ally

Every major law firm has brand guidelines. Yet most decks ignore them. Fonts vary, colours shift, and logos appear at different sizes. To the client, it feels amateur. To you, it feels invisible. But inconsistency erodes confidence.

Use a single PowerPoint template. Set the font stack, colour palette, and master layouts before you start. Lock the template down so no one can override styles. If you update the brand, propagate the changes across all decks instantly.

## Data slides must pass the 3-second test

Clients expect numbers. But they won’t stare at a table for 30 seconds. Make data visual and memorable.

- Replace dense financial tables with sparklines or small bar charts.
- Round numbers to two significant figures. “£3.2bn” reads faster than “£3,187,456,321.”
- Highlight the one number that matters most in your brand colour.

If the client can’t grasp the point in three seconds, the slide fails.

## Delivery trumps design

A great deck in the wrong hands is a wasted opportunity. Train your partners to pause after key slides. Silence sells more than sentences. And always end with a clear next step: “We’ll schedule a kick-off call next Tuesday.” No ambiguity, no follow-up delay.

## Do this today

Open your strongest pitch deck. Does it:

- Start with a client problem?
- Use fewer than 12 slides?
- Rely on visuals over text?
- Match your brand guidelines perfectly?

If not, fix it now. Print it out. Staple it together. Hand it to a non-lawyer colleague. If they can’t summarise your pitch in 30 seconds, rewrite it. Your next client won’t give you a second chance.
