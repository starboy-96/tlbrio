Your brand guidelines live in a PDF on a shared drive that nobody reads. Your team of 120 people is making decks in PowerPoint and half of them use the wrong colour palette. The other half ignore your font rules entirely. This is what happens when you try to maintain brand standards across large teams without a proper system.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: scale breaks everything. When you had 10 people, you could eyeball presentations and nudge them back into shape. Now you have 100+. You cannot physically check every deck. You need a different approach.

## The problem with hoping people care

Most teams start with brand guidelines that look polished and professional. A 40-page PDF with Pantone numbers and breathing room rules. Then they send it to everyone and assume compliance will follow.

It does not.

People are busy. They do not memorise your brand standards. They open PowerPoint, they see a blank slide, and they make choices based on muscle memory or whatever they half-remember from the last meeting. Your brand integrity erodes one deck at a time.

The real issue is friction. If following your brand standards requires effort, people will not do it consistently. The easier path is always the one people take.

## Make compliance the default, not the choice

The smartest teams flip this problem. Instead of asking people to remember and apply standards, they build the standards into the tool itself.

A branded PowerPoint template is not optional formatting guidance. It is the starting point for every single deck. Your team opens PowerPoint and the template is already there. Correct colours. Correct fonts. Correct spacing. They cannot deviate without actively choosing to.

This is not restrictive. This is respectful of people's time.

When you use a proper template system, your team of 100+ people instantly produces consistent work without needing to think about it. New joiners automatically use the right visual language. Remote workers in different offices follow the same standards. Contractors you hire for short projects still represent your brand correctly.

The template becomes your enforcement mechanism without ever nagging anyone.

## Where most templates fail

Standard PowerPoint templates have a fatal flaw: they are static files. Someone designs them, uploads them to the shared drive, and then they age like milk. Six months later, someone updates the logo, but nobody tells the template keeper. The template now shows an old version of your brand.

Your team keeps using it. Your decks become inconsistent because people are working from different template versions.

A better system includes version control and update management. When you change your brand, your templates update automatically for everyone. No manual distribution. No confusion about which version is current.

## The governance conversation nobody enjoys

With 100+ people, someone needs to own brand consistency. This does not mean a gatekeeping role who approves every presentation. That is a bottleneck that slows everyone down and breeds resentment.

Instead, assign someone to maintain the master template and handle updates. Give them authority to make decisions about brand application in edge cases. Train team leads to spot obvious deviations and flag them early.

The goal is prevention, not punishment.

Create a simple feedback loop. If someone makes a deck that badly breaks brand standards, tell them. Show them the correct way. Make it constructive. Most people want to get it right. They just need clear direction and easy tools.

## Audit occasionally, improve continuously

Every quarter, pick three or four decks at random from different teams. Check them against your standards. You will spot patterns. Maybe everyone is using the wrong button style. Maybe presentation titles are inconsistent. Maybe images are not being treated with your brand's visual language.

Use these audits to improve your templates and guidance, not to name and shame individuals.

Research shows that [consistency in visual communication increases brand recognition](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbescommunicationscouncil/2023/07/31/how-visual-branding-impacts-customer-perception/) by a measurable margin. Your effort here has actual business value.

## Start today

If you manage presentations at scale, do this now: audit your current template situation. Is it a single file that never updates? Are multiple versions floating around? Do people even know where to find it?

Fix that first. Make your template the easiest option. Then [watch how brand consistency becomes automatic instead of aspirational](https://tlbr.io/blog).

Your team of 100+ people will still make decks at different speeds and with different levels of design skill. But at least they will all be using the same visual language. That is the win at scale.
