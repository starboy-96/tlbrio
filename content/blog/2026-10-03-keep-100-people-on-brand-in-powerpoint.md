Your brand standards document is probably gathering dust somewhere. A 40-page PDF that nobody reads. That's the real problem with maintaining brand standards across a team of 100+ people in PowerPoint.

You can't enforce consistency through documents. You enforce it through friction. Make the right choice the easiest choice. Make the wrong choice obvious.

## The decay starts small

When you have five people building decks, you notice when someone uses the wrong colour. You say something. It gets fixed. At 100 people, you've lost visibility entirely. Someone's using a gradient that doesn't exist in your brand palette. Another person cropped the logo wrong. A third is mixing two completely different font hierarchies in the same presentation.

Most teams only catch these mistakes during the compliance check right before the board meeting. By then, you're asking people to rebuild slides under deadline pressure. They resent the feedback. The quality suffers anyway.

## Your brand template isn't working hard enough

Here's what most organisations do: they build a PowerPoint template with the logo, colour palette, and fonts locked in. They call it done.

That template is doing about 10% of the work it should be doing. A real brand enforcement system needs to make decisions for people, not just show them options.

Your template should include pre-built slide layouts for every common use case your team actually builds. Quarterly business reviews. Sales pitches. Technical deep dives. Internal communications. Financial reports. Each layout should be so complete that starting from scratch is never the faster option.

When people don't need to design, they can't break the rules.

## Create a living template library

The single best tactic for maintaining brand standards at scale is to stop asking people to build individual slides and start giving them finished components they can remix.

Build a master slide deck that contains every slide type you actually use. Not 12 generic options. Dozens. Maybe 50+. Title slides with your exact hierarchy. Content slides with your table styles pre-formatted. Chart templates with your approved colour schemes baked in. Process diagrams. Timeline slides. Comparison layouts. All of it done right once, then reused infinitely.

When someone needs to add a financial comparison to their deck, they grab the pre-built slide, drop in their numbers, and they're done. The design is already correct. The fonts are right. The spacing is locked.

This sounds like a lot of work upfront. It is. But it's front-loaded work that saves thousands of hours downstream.

## Make updates flow downward, not upward

The moment you change your brand standards, every existing presentation becomes slightly wrong. Most teams accept this. They just let the inconsistency accumulate.

Instead, set up a system where template changes propagate to all open decks. [Find tools that let you push updates](https://tlbr.io/#features) across your entire team's presentations at once. When you refresh the colour palette or adjust the logo sizing, everyone's decks update automatically. Old presentations don't become brand violations just because time passed.

This requires the right infrastructure. Spreadsheet-based templates don't do this. Desktop PowerPoint alone can't do this reliably. But it's worth solving because manual updates will never happen at scale.

## Audit and feedback loops

You need quarterly spot checks. Pick 10 random decks from different teams and audit them properly. Look for font inconsistencies, colour drift, logo sizing, spacing violations. Document what you find and send specific feedback.

People care about what you measure. Make brand consistency something you actually measure and report on.

## Make guidelines actionable, not theoretical

Your brand guidelines should answer specific questions your team actually asks.

Not: "Use our font family across all communications."

Instead: "Use Helvetica Neue for all body copy at minimum 11pt. Use Montserrat bold for section headings at 24pt minimum. Never use more than two fonts in a single deck."

Specific beats inspirational every single time.

## The real rule

You cannot police 100 people into compliance through vigilance and messaging. You can only design systems that make compliance the path of least resistance.

Build your template. Make it complete. Push updates automatically. Audit quarterly. The consistency will follow.
