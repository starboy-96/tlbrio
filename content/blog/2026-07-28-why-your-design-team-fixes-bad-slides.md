# Why your design team is spending too much time fixing other people's slides

Your design team just spent three hours unpicking a slide deck that should have been done in 30 minutes. The fonts don’t match, the layouts are all over the place, and half the charts are broken. Again.

This isn’t a one-off. It’s a pattern. And it’s costing your business serious time and money.

## They’re not designers — and that’s fine

Most people in your organisation aren’t trained designers. They’re finance analysts, engineers, or executives who need to communicate ideas quickly. Asking them to produce pixel-perfect slides is like asking a surgeon to fix your boiler. It’s not their job.

But when their slides look like a toddler’s first attempt, your design team steps in. They rebuild layouts, fix colours, and rescue broken graphics. That’s not design — that’s damage control.

## The real cost isn’t just time

Every hour your team spends correcting slides is an hour they’re *not* working on high-value projects. That’s lost creativity, slower brand rollouts, and frustrated designers who joined to make things look amazing, not babysit bad PowerPoint habits.

And it’s not just productivity that suffers. Poor slides reflect badly on the whole company. Imagine a client presentation riddled with inconsistent fonts and misaligned logos. The client doesn’t think, “Oh, the designer was busy.” They think, “This company doesn’t care.”

## The tools are part of the problem

PowerPoint is powerful, but it’s also a free-for-all. Anyone can drop in a random font, stretch a logo, or copy-paste a chart from Excel. Without guardrails, chaos spreads faster than coffee stains in a meeting room.

Design teams try to fix this by creating templates. But templates only work if people *use* them. If someone ignores the template or tweaks it “just a little,” the whole system unravels.

## Brand consistency is a team sport

Your brand isn’t just a logo. It’s the way every slide looks, sounds, and feels. When 50 people create slides independently, consistency vanishes like a Monday morning coffee spillage.

Design teams waste hours aligning colours, matching fonts, and reworking layouts to match brand guidelines. But if guidelines aren’t easy to follow, they won’t be followed. And if tools don’t enforce them, they’re useless.

## You’re paying twice — once for the slide, once for the fix

Every slide your team rebuilds is a double expense. First, the original creator wasted time making a mess. Then, your designer spent more time cleaning it up. Multiply that by hundreds of slides each year, and you’re looking at a serious bill.

This isn’t sustainable. Design teams aren’t cost centres — they’re value creators. They should be designing new templates, not fixing broken ones.

## What you can do today

Stop expecting non-designers to be designers. Instead, give them the tools and guidance they need to get it right first time.

1. **Simplify your templates.** Strip them back to essential layouts. More options mean more mistakes.
2. **Enforce rules automatically.** Use a PowerPoint add-in that locks fonts, colours, and layouts. No exceptions.
3. **Train, but don’t overload.** Focus on one or two key skills — like using the right template and avoiding rogue fonts.
4. **Measure the waste.** Track how many hours your team spends fixing slides. Show the cost to leadership. Numbers get attention.

Your design team didn’t join to be slide janitors. They joined to make your brand shine. It’s time to give them the tools to do just that.
