Your design team spent three months nailing your brand guidelines. Then you watched 120 people ignore them in PowerPoint.

This is not a soft problem. Brand inconsistency erodes trust. Clients see five different colour palettes in your deck. Your fonts shift mid-presentation. Stakeholders waste time reformatting instead of reviewing content. By the time you spot the damage, 40 decks are already live.

The issue is simple: you told people what to do. You did not make it easy to do it.

Brand standards only work at scale when you remove choice from the equation. You need a centralised system that enforces consistency without requiring discipline from busy people who have actual work to do.

## Start with a bulletproof template library

Build one master template that lives in a shared location everyone accesses. Not emailed around. Not stored on someone's desktop. One source of truth. Every approved colour, font, spacing rule, and layout option should live there. When marketing updates the logo, it updates everywhere automatically.

Make variations for your most common use cases: investor presentations, internal updates, sales pitches, training decks. People will work faster with templates tailored to what they actually do.

Then lock the template. Use slide masters to prevent accidental font swaps. Restrict what can be edited. You want people filling in content, not redesigning slides.

Next, assign a small governance team. Two or three people who review new decks before they go external. This is not bureaucracy. This is quality control. Give them clear criteria for approval: fonts match the master, colours are from the brand palette, imagery follows your guidelines.

Build accountability into your review workflow. Make the approval process part of your normal sign-off cycle, not an afterthought.

Finally, use [a tool that syncs your templates and enforces brand rules](https://tlbr.io/#features) across your team. Manual policing does not scale past 20 people. You need software that updates templates instantly and flags brand violations before anyone presents.

[Research shows](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesbusinesscouncil/2022/03/10/consistent-branding-across-all-platforms/) consistent branding increases revenue recognition by up to 80%. But consistency only happens when it is the path of least resistance.

Make following your brand standards easier than breaking them. That is the whole game.
