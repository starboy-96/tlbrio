Your marketing team just sent you a deck. The logo is the wrong size. The colours don't match your brand guidelines. The font is something nobody approved. And this is your fifth review today.

This is what happens when you let 100+ people loose in PowerPoint without proper guardrails. Brand standards collapse not because people are careless, but because consistency is hard to enforce at scale. Everyone interprets the guidelines differently. Someone finds an old template. A contractor uses whatever they want. Suddenly you have fifty versions of your brand floating around.

The real problem is that brand guidelines live in a document nobody reads. They sit in a shared drive gathering dust whilst your team builds decks from scratch or copies old ones they find lying around. You need systems that make doing it right easier than doing it wrong.

Start with a master template. Not a suggestion. A working template that everyone actually uses. Your colours are locked in. Your fonts are embedded. Your logo placement is fixed. When someone opens the file, they see the right starting point immediately. They cannot accidentally use Helvetica because Helvetica does not exist in that file.

Then make it accessible. Drop it somewhere your team finds it without asking. Build a quick reference guide that shows correct usage in three minutes, not thirty pages. Link it from your intranet. Email it when new people join.

The hard part is enforcement. [Use a tool that lets you centralise your brand assets](https://tlbr.io/#features) so your team pulls from one approved library instead of guessing. Track which decks use which templates. Make it clear when someone goes off-brand, and make it easy to fix.

Finally, spot-check early. Review decks before they leave your team. Catch the mistakes when they are ten slides, not a hundred. This takes thirty seconds per deck if you catch it early.

[Research from the Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2016/03/the-brand-new-rules-of-organizational-culture) shows that brand consistency builds trust and revenue. You cannot maintain that consistency by hoping. You maintain it by removing the option to fail.

Stop fighting brand decay with emails and spreadsheets. Build a system that makes your standards the default.
