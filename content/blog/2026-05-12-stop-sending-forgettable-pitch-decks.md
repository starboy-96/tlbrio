# Stop sending forgettable pitch decks

Your last pitch deck? Probably a victim of “design by committee.” Too many cooks added slides, swapped fonts, and buried your real message under jargon. The result? A 20-slide snoozefest that clients skim and forget.

It doesn’t have to be this way. Small tweaks in structure, design, and delivery can turn a forgettable deck into one that wins mandates.

## Start with a client-centric roadmap

Most pitches read like internal strategy documents. Clients don’t care about your firm’s heritage. They care about one thing: how you’ll solve their problem.

So structure your deck around their journey:

- **Problem:** What keeps them up at night? Use their language, not legalese.
- **Solution:** Show your approach in three clear steps. Clients want process, not promises.
- **Proof:** Clients want names, case studies, and quantifiable outcomes.
- **Team:** Introduce the people who’ll actually do the work, not just bios.
- **Next steps:** Make it stupidly easy to say yes. Include a one-pager attachment.

This isn’t creative writing. It’s client psychology.

## Kill the slide bloat before it kills your pitch

The average law firm pitch deck has 28 slides. The average client reads seven.

That math hurts.

Trim ruthlessly:

- Remove the “Our Firm” slides unless they’re directly relevant to the client’s problem.
- Cut the “Global Reach” map if the client only operates in the UK.
- Delete partner photos with gavel graphics. Clients don’t care how many awards you’ve won; they care who’ll handle their file.
- Avoid walls of text. Use 30-point fonts and one idea per slide.

If a slide doesn’t serve the client’s decision, delete it. No exceptions.

## Use visuals that reinforce credibility

Lawyers love bullet points. Clients hate them.

Instead, use visuals that tell the story:

- **Timeline diagrams** to show your approach.
- **Icons** to signal key themes (e.g., a gavel for “litigation,” a handshake for “partnership”).
- **Headshots with clear roles** so clients know who’s who.
- **Minimal charts** with one data point each. If you must show revenue growth, use a clean bar chart—not a 3D pie.

Your visuals must do the talking while you do the selling.

## Speak like a human, not a law firm robot

Legal language is the fastest way to lose a client. Swap “pursuant to Section X” with “based on the contract terms.” Replace “leveraging our cross-jurisdictional expertise” with “we’ve handled cases like yours in three countries.”

Use the client’s industry terms. If you’re pitching a fintech client, talk about “regulatory sandboxes,” not “financial services regulation.” Mirror their language to build trust instantly.

## Make the deck work for your team

A great pitch deck isn’t just for clients. It’s your internal playbook.

- Use consistent layouts so anyone can present without fumbling.
- Add speaker notes with key client questions and your answers.
- Build a “leave-behind” one-pager that summarises the pitch in plain English.
- Store the deck in a shared library so partners use the same version.

Consistency builds confidence. Chaos kills deals.

## Test your deck before it meets the client

Practice your pitch with someone outside legal. If they can’t summarise your key points in 30 seconds, your deck is still too complicated.

Ask them:

- What’s the main problem we’re solving?
- Who’s on the team?
- Why should they pick us?

If they hesitate, simplify.

## Fix your pitch deck today

Open your latest pitch deck now. Count the slides. If it’s over 15, delete half. Replace three bullet points with a visual. Swap one jargon phrase with the client’s language.

Then practise it twice. Once alone. Once in front of a sceptical colleague.

Do this today and your next pitch will stand out—not because it’s flashy, but because it’s clear, concise, and client-focused. That’s how you win mandates, not just meetings.
