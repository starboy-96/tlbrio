# Fix these 5 PowerPoint alignment fails in one click

You’ve spent hours polishing your deck, only to spot a crooked line or misaligned bullet point two slides before the big meeting. It happens to everyone. The real crime isn’t the mistake—it’s the time you waste fixing it.

Alignment in PowerPoint isn’t just about looking neat. It’s about credibility. A misaligned shape screams “amateur” faster than a typo. Worse, inconsistent spacing breaks your brand’s rhythm, distracting your audience from your message. Here are the five alignment mistakes that trip up even seasoned presenters—and how to banish them for good.

## 1. Objects fighting the baseline

Text boxes and shapes often ignore the slide’s invisible baseline grid, floating aimlessly above or below it. The result? A deck that feels untethered, like a picture hanging crooked on the wall.

Align text to the baseline by selecting your text box and using PowerPoint’s baseline grid. But don’t rely on dragging—the snap isn’t precise enough. Instead, use an alignment tool that locks objects to the grid automatically.

## 2. Off-centre titles breaking the slide

A title that’s one pixel off centre unbalances the whole slide. Your eye knows it’s wrong, even if you can’t articulate why.

Use a horizontal alignment tool to centre titles instantly. Look for one that centres relative to the slide or master layout—not just the selected object. That tiny shift makes your deck feel polished, not fussy.

## 3. Inconsistent spacing between shapes

Rows of icons or stacked text boxes end up with uneven gaps. You tweak one, then another, then a third—and suddenly your spacing looks like a staircase.

Group the shapes first, then use a vertical or horizontal distribution tool. It spaces them evenly in one click. No more manual nudging, no more guessing.

## 4. Text boxes ignoring the master layout

You copy a slide, change the title, and suddenly your body text is a millimetre higher than it should be. The master layout is ignored, and your brand guide is broken.

Apply a master layout tool that snaps objects back to their defined positions. It’s not about reshaping—it’s about enforcing consistency across every slide.

## 5. Rotated objects throwing off the grid

Rotated text boxes or shapes break the alignment grid entirely. They’re no longer horizontal or vertical, so your eye registers them as chaotic.

Use an alignment tool that realigns rotated objects to the nearest gridline or baseline. It keeps the visual order intact without flattening your design.
