# Your design team wastes half their week fixing bad slides

Your design team just spent three hours fixing a quarterly update deck because someone used 12 different fonts and 16 shades of blue. Again.

And you can’t blame them. Fixing other people’s slides is what design teams do. But here’s the truth: it shouldn’t be. Every hour they spend untangling someone else’s slide chaos is an hour not spent on strategy, brand, or impact.

So why is this happening? And more importantly, how do you stop it?

## The root cause: PowerPoint is the world’s most permissive design tool

PowerPoint lets anyone do anything. Want Comic Sans on a dark-blue background? Done. Slide with 47 bullet points? No problem. Your design team gets the deck after it’s been ‘finished’ by someone who thinks design is about filling space, not clarity.

The result? A deck that looks like a ransom note, and a design team that has to rebuild it from scratch.

Worse, the damage isn’t just aesthetic. Poorly designed slides waste time in meetings. They confuse stakeholders. They make your brand look inconsistent. And every time your design team fixes one, they reinforce the cycle: people think design is just ‘prettying up’ slides, not shaping how your message lands.

## The hidden cost isn’t just time—it’s credibility

How much time are you really losing? Research from McKinsey shows that knowledge workers waste up to 2.5 hours a day on low-value tasks like reformatting slides. For a team of eight designers, that’s 20 hours a week—half a designer’s time, vanished.

But the real cost is credibility. When your brand’s decks look inconsistent, your audience doesn’t trust the message. And when your design team is always in cleanup mode, they can’t push the needle on design systems, accessibility, or visual storytelling.

The problem compounds. The more your team fixes, the more others expect them to fix. And the less they focus on what actually moves the business forward.

## Brand consistency? Almost certainly broken

Most enterprise teams think their brand is consistent until they open 20 decks from different regions. Logos stretched. Fonts swapped. Colours misused. Templates ignored.

This isn’t laziness. It’s lack of guardrails. PowerPoint gives infinite freedom, and most people treat it like a blank canvas. Without real-time brand checks, mistakes spread like wildfire.

Your design team knows this. They see it every week. But they’re too busy fixing slides to build the systems that prevent the fixes.

## The fix starts with giving your team the right tools

You can’t stop people from using PowerPoint. But you can stop them from breaking it.

Start by giving your design team tools that enforce consistency in real time. Not a PDF guideline buried in a shared drive. Not a once-a-year brand workshop. Something that lives inside PowerPoint and works like a spellchecker—for design.

For example, a PowerPoint add-in that automatically checks fonts, colours, logos, and layout as people build slides. One that flags deviations before they spread. One that turns every slide into a branded, polished asset—without your team lifting a finger.

## Small steps, big change

You won’t fix this overnight. But you can start today.

First, audit how much time your team spends fixing slides. Track it for a week. You’ll be shocked.

Second, empower your designers to say no. Not in a gatekeeping way. But in a coaching way. Teach presenters how to use templates. Show them what a well-structured slide looks like. Make it easy for them to succeed.

Third, invest in one tool that enforces brand rules in PowerPoint. Something that runs in the background, like a design linter. It doesn’t replace your team. It enables them.

## The bottom line

Your design team shouldn’t be a slide repair service. They should be brand builders, story architects, and visual strategists.

Every hour they spend fixing someone else’s deck is a step backward for your brand and your business.

So stop the cycle. Give them the tools. Set the rules. And free them to do what they do best.
