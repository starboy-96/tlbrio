## Why alignment matters (and why no one gets it right)

Misaligned elements in a PowerPoint deck aren’t just an aesthetic problem—they erode trust. When shapes, text boxes, or icons drift even a few pixels off-grid, your audience subconsciously questions the professionalism of the content. Worse, manual alignment fixes can take hours across large decks. The good news? These mistakes are easy to spot and fix—if you know where to look.

## Mistake 1: Inconsistent spacing around text

Text-heavy slides often suffer from inconsistent margins, leading to a cluttered or disjointed appearance. Either text boxes are crammed too close to the slide edge, or gaps vary wildly between bullet points and titles. This disrupts the visual flow and makes the deck look unpolished.

**Fix:** Use a grid-based layout or a slide master with fixed spacing. Adjust the slide master margins once, then apply them across the entire deck. For existing decks, select all text boxes and apply consistent padding using a ruler or grid overlay.

## Mistake 2: Off-centre objects on otherwise symmetrical slides

Even when most elements are aligned, a single off-centre icon, chart, or logo can throw off the entire slide. This usually happens when shapes are copied from different sources or pasted without checking positioning. The result? A subtle imbalance that distracts viewers.

**Fix:** Enable alignment guides (View > Guides) and snap objects to the centre. For precise centring, select all relevant objects, then use the “Align Centre” and “Distribute Vertically” tools. Alternatively, use a master slide with pre-placed placeholders to prevent drift.

## Mistake 3: Uneven baselines in multi-line text

When bullet points or paragraphs have inconsistent baseline spacing, the text appears jagged. This often occurs when line spacing is adjusted manually or when text is pasted from Word without clearing formatting. It breaks rhythm and makes the slide harder to read.

**Fix:** Reset all text to the default line spacing (usually 1.0 or 1.5). Use the paragraph formatting panel to set consistent spacing before and after. For brand decks, define these values in the slide master to ensure consistency across all presenters.

## Mistake 4: Floating elements outside the grid

Shapes or images placed “just so” outside the grid can create visual tension. While intentional breaks can work in design-heavy decks, they’re risky in professional presentations where consistency is key. These elements often get nudged accidentally during editing, leading to hidden misalignments.

**Fix:** Always enable the grid (View > Grid and Guides) and set it to 5mm or 10mm increments. Use “Snap to Grid” to lock elements in place. For complex slides, create a hidden grid layer in the master slide to guide placement.

## Mistake 5: Overlapping or misaligned brand assets

Logos, icons, and branded graphics often end up misaligned due to inconsistent scaling or manual positioning. If your brand guidelines specify exact margins or alignment rules, even small deviations can make the deck feel unprofessional.

**Fix:** Use a branded template with locked placeholders for logos and icons. If updating an existing deck, select all branded assets and apply the same alignment and spacing using a style guide overlay. For large teams, consider a shared template with built-in alignment rules.
