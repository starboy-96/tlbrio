## Why alignment errors make your deck look amateur

Every slide in your deck is either building trust or eroding it. Misaligned text boxes, off-grid shapes, and uneven spacing scream “rushed” rather than “rigorous.” Research by the Nielsen Norman Group shows users notice alignment issues in under a second, forming an immediate impression of quality. Whether you’re in auditing, sales, or HR, your audience expects precision. These five alignment mistakes are the most common offenders—and the fastest to fix.

## 1. Off-centre text blocks

Left-aligned text is the default for good reason: it’s scannable and professional. Yet teams still drag text boxes by eye, leaving uneven margins. A 2 mm shift on one slide becomes a 2 cm drift across 20 slides. The result? A disjointed, unpolished look.

**Fix it in one click:** Use the ruler and grid to snap text to a master margin, or apply a consistent text box style. For enterprise teams, [brand-controlled styles](https://tlbr.io/#features) ensure every text block aligns to your grid—no eyeballing.

## 2. Ragged edges on grouped objects

You’ve aligned three shapes perfectly, but the group floats 1 mm above the baseline. Zoom in: the edges aren’t crisp. This happens when objects are nudged manually or pasted from different decks. Over 60 % of decks reviewed by presentation agencies show this flaw.

**Fix it in one click:** Select all objects, use “Align to Slide” then “Distribute Vertically,” then “Align Middle.” For enterprise teams, [apply once and reuse](https://tlbr.io/#demo): store your layout as a template and enforce alignment across every deck.

## 3. Inconsistent spacing between bullet points

Your bullet spacing varies from 4.2 pt to 6.8 pt. It’s subtle, but the human eye detects inconsistency faster than you think. Uneven spacing disrupts the vertical rhythm, making content harder to scan—especially on mobile or in client presentations.

**Fix it in one click:** Use paragraph spacing set to exact values (e.g., 6 pt after, 0 pt before). Update the style once, and every new slide inherits the spacing automatically. For teams managing multiple decks, [centralise your spacing rules](https://tlbr.io/#pricing) to eliminate drift.

## 4. Floating icons or logos off the baseline

Icons pasted from different sources rarely sit on the same baseline. A 1 mm drop makes your brand look sloppy. In finance and consulting, where attention to detail is currency, this is a credibility killer.

**Fix it in one click:** Align icons to the text baseline using the “Align Bottom” command. Or, define an icon style with a fixed bottom margin. Enterprise teams can enforce this globally through [brand style packs](https://tlbr.io/#features), ensuring every icon sits flush.

## 5. Misaligned slide numbers or footers

Slide numbers that sit 2 mm left or right of the master footer break the visual hierarchy. Worse, they look like an afterthought—never a good sign in board decks or investor pitches.

**Fix it in one click:** Lock footer elements to the master slide and use the “Align Left” or “Align Right” commands. For teams that update decks frequently, [centralise footers](https://tlbr.io/#demo) so one change propagates across all slides.
