PowerPoint’s alignment guides feel helpful until your slide ends up looking like a drunk scatter plot.

Every brand has a set of alignment rules: margins, gutters, baseline grids. Yet teams still send decks where bullet points float randomly or logos drift three pixels from the left edge. These aren’t small slips. They’re silent brand betrayals that make your company look sloppy under scrutiny.

Here are the five alignment mistakes everyone makes in PowerPoint and how to fix them in one click.

## Your shapes are centre-aligned when they should be left

Centre alignment is PowerPoint’s default. It’s also the most common reason your slides feel amateur.

Left alignment is the safe choice for text boxes, charts, and callout shapes. It keeps content anchored and easy to scan. Centre alignment works only for titles or isolated elements—not for the building blocks of your slide.

PowerPoint’s alignment tools don’t remember your brand’s left edge. Every time you copy a shape, it snaps back to centre. The fix? Use a grid or ruler guide tied to your master layout. Or use an add-in that locks your brand’s left margin and applies it in one click.

## Your bullet points don’t sit on the same baseline

PowerPoint lets you resize text boxes without adjusting the baseline. The result? Bullets float at different heights across the deck.

This breaks visual rhythm. It screams “designed by committee” even when your message is sharp. The eye notices inconsistency before it reads the words.

Use a baseline grid or enable PowerPoint’s baseline guides. Make sure every bullet line aligns with the grid. If you’re working across teams, enforce this with a template add-in that snaps body text to a shared baseline in one click.

## Your logos drift from the required position

Logos are brand DNA. Yet they drift left or right because PowerPoint’s alignment ignores your brand book.

A logo 2 pixels off centre doesn’t seem like a big deal. But when every slide in a 50-slide deck has the same tiny error, it compounds into visible sloppiness. Stakeholders notice. Clients feel uneasy. You lose credibility without knowing why.

Lock your logo position in the master slide. Then use a template tool that pins logos to exact coordinates on every slide. It takes one click to reset a misaligned logo across a deck.

## Your charts inherit inconsistent padding

PowerPoint’s default chart padding is too wide. Widen it further and your chart drifts from the left margin. Copy the chart to another slide and the padding resets.

This breaks brand consistency faster than inconsistent fonts. Your charts should feel like part of the same system, not random inserts.

Use a chart template that locks padding and margins. Apply it in one click to every new chart. No manual resizing. No guessing.

## Your icons don’t align with text or shapes

Icons are visual punctuation. When they’re misaligned, the slide feels chaotic.

PowerPoint doesn’t treat icons as part of the layout. They inherit the alignment of their parent shape or text box. Move the parent and the icon drifts. Copy the icon to another slide and it snaps to a different base.

Use an icon library tied to your brand alignment rules. Position icons relative to text baselines and left margins. Apply alignment in one click to restore order.

Fix these five mistakes today

Alignment isn’t decoration. It’s the silent ambassador of your brand. Every misaligned bullet or drifting logo chips away at trust before you’ve even spoken.
