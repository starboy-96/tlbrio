**The three-option decision**

When a chart (or table, or other object) is too large for its intended space, you have three genuine options. Each trades something off. Pick based on what matters most to your audience and deadline.

**Option 1: Reduce the data series**

Remove one or more lines, columns or categories from the chart itself. The chart gets smaller because it shows less information.

*Best for:* Comparing two or three things clearly rather than overwhelming the viewer with eight overlapping lines.

*Cost:* You lose detail. Make sure the removed data won't be questioned in the room.

*Example:* A sales chart showing results for 12 regions. You need space to add interpretation below. Keep only the top three regions and national average. Reduces visual clutter and fits the space without shrinking the font.

**Option 2: Shrink the chart, keep all data**

Make the whole chart smaller while keeping every data series. Fonts scale down with it.

*Best for:* When the data matters but the slide has room elsewhere, or when you're comparing many items and omitting any would be unfair.

*Cost:* Smaller fonts become harder to read, especially from the back of the room or on a laptop screen. Check readability at actual viewing distance first.

*Example:* A detailed comparison of 20 competitors across five metrics. You need the full picture to justify a decision. Shrink the chart to 60% of its original size, then add a text summary above it that names the three most important findings. The chart becomes supporting detail, not the main message.

**Option 3: Move or split the chart**

Put the chart on its own slide, or split it into two smaller charts across two slides.

*Best for:* When the data is genuinely complex and deserves focus, or when you need space for a decision or next steps.

*Cost:* Uses more slides. Make sure each one earns its place.

Before you choose, ask: does the person in the room need to see all this data at once? If yes, pick Option 1 or 3. If the full detail is reference material, Option 2 works.

**Verify readability**

Whichever option you choose, view the slide at the actual presentation distance. Fonts under 11pt become difficult to read from more than two metres away. If you are shrinking or keeping a complex chart, test it.
