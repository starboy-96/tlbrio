Your brand looks different in every deck. Someone's using the old logo. Someone else picked a font that was never approved. A third person invented a colour that doesn't exist in your guidelines. And by the time you notice, you've got 47 presentations floating around that don't represent your company.

This is what happens when you try to maintain brand standards across a team of 100+ people in PowerPoint without a system. It's not laziness. It's not wilful disobedience. It's chaos. And it scales quickly.

## The Problem Isn't Your Standards. It's Friction.

You probably have brand guidelines. They're probably good. They sit in a document somewhere that people bookmark, forget about, then can't find when they need them.

Even when people want to follow your standards, the friction is too high. They need to download a logo. Hunt for the right font. Match a specific blue from a PDF. Open an old deck to copy the layout. By the time they've done all that, they've already made five decisions that break your brand.

And you can't blame them. They've got a presentation due in three hours.

## Make Standards Easier Than Breaking Them

This is the core principle. Your team will follow brand standards when doing so costs less time and effort than ignoring them.

The easiest way to do this is templates. Not templates as an afterthought. Templates as your default way of working.

Build templates that solve real problems your team faces. If your sales team is always creating product comparison slides, give them a template with the comparisons pre-built. If your finance team presents the same metrics every quarter, create a template that pulls those in automatically. Templates aren't about restriction. They're about removing decisions.

Make these templates visible and easy to find. Store them centrally. Make them the first thing people see when they open PowerPoint. If finding a template is harder than starting from a blank slide, you've failed.

## Establish a Single Source of Truth for Your Brand

Everyone should have access to one place where your brand assets live. Not three places. Not email attachments. One place.

This should include colours in a format PowerPoint understands. Fonts you've licensed or approved. Logo files in multiple variations. Typography rules with examples. And someone responsible for keeping it current.

When you update your brand, people need to know immediately. When Microsoft releases a new Windows version that breaks your font rendering, you need to communicate the fix. When marketing approves a new colour, designers need to access it that day.

A shared drive with folders works. A dedicated brand portal works better. What matters is that it's the first place people check, not the last.

## Create a Review Process That Doesn't Kill Momentum

Large teams need governance. But governance can strangle productivity if you get it wrong.

You don't need to review every slide. You need to review key decks. Client presentations. Investor meetings. Board updates. Anything external facing. Create a simple checklist for reviewers. Logo treatment. Colour usage. Font families. Spacing consistency. Make reviews take 15 minutes, not 15 days.

Build that review time into your project timelines. If a deck needs approval, people should expect a two-day turnaround, not two weeks. Speed up the process and people will actually use it.

## Use Tools That Enforce Standards Automatically

Manual enforcement doesn't scale to 100+ people. You need technology that makes breaking standards difficult or impossible.

Branded PowerPoint add-ins let you build approved layouts directly into the application. [Your team works from templates that lock down fonts, colours, and spacing](https://tlbr.io/#features) without making slides feel rigid. When someone wants to deviate, they see why the standard exists before they do it.

This removes the need for constant policing. Your standards are built into the tools themselves.

## Start With Your Heaviest Users

You don't need to convince everyone on day one. Start with your largest internal audience. Sales. Marketing. Finance. Whoever creates the most decks.

Get them to adoption first. Let other teams see the efficiency gains. Let them see how much faster a presentation comes together when you don't have to make a hundred design decisions. Momentum builds from success, not from mandate.

## Your Takeaway

Start today: pick one team that creates the most presentations. Build them a single template that solves their biggest problem. Store it somewhere dead simple. Give it a two-week trial. Measure whether they created decks faster. If they did, you've found your model. Scale it from there.

Brand standards across 100+ people work when you make compliance easier than the alternative. Templates, centralised assets, light-touch review, and the right tools do that.
